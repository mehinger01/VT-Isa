Method 1

Before class: Prime the lesson

The goal is not to teach yourself everything. Spend a few minutes making the material familiar so class is your second exposure, not your first.

The four-minute preview

  1. Scan the title, headings, examples, and formulas.
  2. Write what you already know.
  3. Make one prediction.
  4. Write one question you want class to answer.

Keep it small

A useful preview should usually take 3–5 minutes. Stop before it becomes a separate homework assignment.

Method 2

During class: Capture the structure

Do not copy every sentence. Record the parts you will need to understand and reconstruct the lesson later.

The five-part note system

  1. Main idea: What is the teacher trying to show?
  2. Why it works: What reasoning connects the steps?
  3. Example: What model problem demonstrates it?
  4. Warning: What mistake or exception matters?
  5. Question: Where is your understanding incomplete?

Practice lesson: The discriminant

The discriminant is the expression under the square root in the quadratic formula.

  • Δ > 0: two distinct real roots; two x-intercepts.
  • Δ = 0: one repeated real root; the graph touches the x-axis once.
  • Δ < 0: two nonreal complex roots; no x-intercepts.

Important: A negative discriminant means no real roots—not no solutions.

Method 3

After class: Retrieve, check, and ask

Do not begin by rereading. First, close the notes and produce what you remember. Then compare, correct, and identify any question that remains.

The ten-minute follow-up

  1. Close the notes.
  2. Write the main idea from memory.
  3. Do one example without help.
  4. Open the notes and correct the result.
  5. Write one precise teacher question if needed.
