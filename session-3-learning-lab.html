Stage 6 · Phase 3B

Repair the model

Compare your reconstruction with the reference. Identify the precise gap rather than merely copying the right answer.

Reference model

  • Δ = b² − 4ac.
  • Δ > 0: two distinct real roots; two x-intercepts.
  • Δ = 0: one repeated real root; the parabola touches the x-axis once.
  • Δ < 0: two nonreal complex roots; no x-intercepts.
  • The sign matters because Δ sits under the square root in the quadratic formula.

Retrieval answers

  1. Δ = 9: two distinct real roots.
  2. Δ = 0: one repeated real root.
  3. Δ = −40: two nonreal complex roots.
  4. Two, one tangent, and zero x-intercepts respectively.
  5. Complex solutions still exist even when real x-intercepts do not.

Stage 7 · Transfer

Can the learning travel?

Apply the discriminant when the numbers, representation, or question changes.

Challenge 1: Construct

Create a quadratic equation with exactly one repeated real root. Verify it using the discriminant.

Challenge 2: Change one coefficient

Start with x² + 4x + 1 = 0. Change only c so the equation has no real roots. Explain the condition your new value must satisfy.

Challenge 3: Parameter reasoning

For x² + kx + 9 = 0, determine the values of k that produce two distinct real roots.

Challenge 4: Diagnose the claim

A student says, “A negative discriminant means the equation has no answers.” Correct the claim precisely.
