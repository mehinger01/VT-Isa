July 29, 2026

Session 2 — Simplifying the system and protecting energy

Calendar Systems

We mapped the current setup

Isa currently uses several calendars for school, activities, family plans, and personal commitments. We identified where information overlaps and where the system creates extra work.

Decision

A practical calendar workaround

The school calendar does not appear to offer a direct export that solves everything automatically. The current workaround is to export it manually, import it into Isa's personal calendar, and then share the needed information with the family calendar.

Energy

Reset before homework

Going directly from a full school day into demanding homework can lead to low-quality work because the energy tank is already depleted. We designed the idea of a short, intentional after-school reset before beginning the next work block.

Performance Routine

50 minutes on, 10 minutes off

We introduced a focused work rhythm: work for 50 minutes, then take a real 10-minute break. The goal is not to grind longer; it is to maintain strong attention and energy across the evening.

Key decisions and follow-through

  • Continue reducing unnecessary overlap between calendars.
  • Test the manual school-calendar import process and determine how often it needs to be repeated.
  • Build a repeatable after-school reset that feels restorative rather than distracting.
  • Experiment with the 50/10 routine during a substantial study task.
  • Use the website tools to make the system visible and easier to maintain.

July 22, 2026

Session 1 — Understanding the current operating system

Strengths

Highly organized and highly driven

Isa already has strong planning habits, a detailed homework task manager, and a serious commitment to doing well. The goal is not to replace those strengths, but to make them easier to sustain.

System Audit

Six calendars create friction

The current system contains multiple calendars serving different purposes. Each one makes sense individually, but together they create duplication, extra checking, and the possibility that something gets missed.

Emerging Need

Recovery is part of performance

Strong organization alone cannot solve an energy problem. We identified the need for planned resets so that demanding days do not require Isa to operate at full intensity without recovery.

Mindset

Done is a decision

Not every assignment deserves showcase-level effort. A major focus will be deciding what “done” looks like before beginning, so effort matches the importance of the task.

Initial priorities

  • Map every calendar and define its exact job.
  • Choose clearer sources of truth for commitments and assignments.
  • Notice when energy is dropping before productivity collapses.
  • Create reset routines that can be used consistently.
  • Practice matching effort level to the actual value of the assignment.

Operating principle

High performance should be sustainable.

The system should reduce mental load, protect energy, and make good decisions easier—especially on busy or tired days.
