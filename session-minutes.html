July 29, 2026

Session 2 — Simplifying your system and protecting your energy

Calendar Systems

You mapped your current setup

You use several calendars for school, activities, family plans, and personal commitments. We identified where information overlaps and where the system creates extra work.

Decision

A practical calendar workaround

Your school calendar does not appear to offer a direct export that solves everything automatically. The current workaround is to export it manually, import it into your personal calendar, and then share the needed information with the family calendar.

Energy

Reset before homework

Going directly from a full school day into demanding homework can produce lower-quality work when your energy tank is already depleted. You identified the value of a short, intentional after-school reset before beginning the next work block.

Performance Routine

50 minutes on, 10 minutes off

You introduced a focused work rhythm: work for 50 minutes, then take a real 10-minute break. The goal is not to grind longer; it is to maintain stronger attention and energy across the evening.

Your follow-through

  • Keep reducing unnecessary overlap between calendars.
  • Test the manual school-calendar import process and decide how often it needs to be repeated.
  • Build an after-school reset that feels restorative rather than distracting.
  • Experiment with the 50/10 routine during a substantial study task.
  • Use your website tools to make the system visible and easier to maintain.

July 22, 2026

Session 1 — Understanding your current operating system

Strengths

You already have strong systems

You came in with strong planning habits, a detailed homework task manager, and a serious commitment to doing well. The goal is not to replace those strengths, but to make them easier to sustain.

System Audit

Six calendars create friction

Your calendars each serve a purpose, but together they create duplication, extra checking, and more chances for something to get missed.

Energy

Recovery is part of performance

Strong organization alone cannot solve an energy problem. Planned resets can help demanding days stay sustainable instead of requiring full intensity from start to finish.

Mindset

Done is a decision

Not every assignment deserves showcase-level effort. One of your goals is to decide what “done” looks like before beginning so your effort matches the importance of the task.

Your starting priorities

  • Map every calendar and define its exact job.
  • Choose clearer sources of truth for commitments and assignments.
  • Notice when your energy is dropping before productivity collapses.
  • Create reset routines you can use consistently.
  • Practice matching effort level to the actual value of the assignment.

Operating principle

High performance should be sustainable.

Your system should reduce mental load, protect your energy, and make good decisions easier—especially on busy or tired days.
