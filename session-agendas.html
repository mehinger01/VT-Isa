5–20 minutes

Build your Assignment Launch Protocol

Use this five-question routine whenever an assignment feels vague, large, or hard to start:

  1. What is the final product? What exactly has to be submitted or demonstrated?
  2. What controls the grade? What do the directions, rubric, format, and deadline actually require?
  3. What materials are needed? Open only what you need to begin.
  4. What is the first visible action? Choose something concrete you can actually do.
  5. What can happen in five minutes? Start before the whole plan feels perfect.

You’ll practice this first with a clearly defined assignment, then with a more complicated one where some decisions are still unclear.

AP U.S. History Reading Lab

Use the Period 6 passage, Industrialization and the Remaking of American Society, 1865–1900, to practice a three-pass reading method.

Pass 1 — Map the structure

Read the title and first sentence of each paragraph. Predict how the passage is organized and what argument it may be building.

Pass 2 — Frame the argument

Read the introduction and conclusion closely. Write the central claim in one sentence.

Pass 3 — Gather evidence

Read the full passage for causes, effects, contrasting experiences, labor-capital conflict, urban change, and qualifying language.

Your target: state the argument, select three strong pieces of evidence, and identify the tension that makes the interpretation more complex.

42–55 minutes

Turn Reading into an AP-Style Response

Use this prompt:

Evaluate the extent to which industrialization transformed American society between 1865 and 1900.

Build your response in four moves:

  1. Write a defensible claim that directly answers “to what extent.”
  2. Select two pieces of historical evidence from the passage.
  3. Explain how each piece of evidence supports your claim.
  4. Add a complexity sentence showing that effects varied by class, race, gender, region, or relationship to industrial power.

You do not need to write a full essay. The goal is to practice moving efficiently from comprehension to argument.
