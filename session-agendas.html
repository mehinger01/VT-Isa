Set the performance target

Introduce the session’s two questions:

  • How can Isa move from seeing an assignment to beginning meaningful work quickly?
  • How can she extract the argument and useful evidence from difficult AP-level reading without treating every sentence equally?

Keep this brief. Calendar consolidation, reset routines, and work cycles have already been addressed and should not be retaught.

5–20 minutes

Build the Assignment Launch Protocol

Teach and personalize a five-question starting routine:

  1. What is the final product? Identify exactly what must be submitted or demonstrated.
  2. What controls the grade? Locate the directions, rubric, required components, format, and deadline.
  3. What materials are needed? Open only what is required to begin.
  4. What is the first visible action? Choose a concrete action rather than a broad intention.
  5. What can happen in five minutes? Use a short launch sprint to create momentum.

Practice twice: first with a clearly defined assignment, then with a vague or complex assignment that requires Isa to make decisions despite incomplete clarity.

New AP U.S. History Reading Lab

Use the new Period 6 passage, Industrialization and the Remaking of American Society, 1865–1900.

Pass 1 — Map the structure

Read the title and first sentence of each paragraph. Predict the passage’s organization and likely argument.

Pass 2 — Frame the argument

Read the introduction and conclusion closely. Write the central claim in one sentence.

Pass 3 — Gather evidence

Read the full passage for causes, effects, contrasting experiences, labor-capital conflict, urban change, and qualifying language.

Success criteria: Isa can state the argument, select three strong pieces of evidence, and identify the tension that makes the interpretation more complex.

42–55 minutes

Turn Reading into an AP-Style Response

Use the prompt:

Evaluate the extent to which industrialization transformed American society between 1865 and 1900.

Build the response in four moves:

  1. Write a defensible claim that directly answers “to what extent.”
  2. Select two pieces of historical evidence from the passage.
  3. Explain how each piece of evidence supports the claim.
  4. Add a complexity sentence showing that effects varied by class, race, gender, region, or relationship to industrial power.

The emphasis is not writing a full essay. It is practicing the transition from comprehension to argument.

55–60 minutes

Refine and keep the tools

Isa rewrites the Assignment Launch Protocol in language she would actually use and identifies one upcoming school task where it could help.

Close with two takeaways:

  • Starting principle: Understand the target, choose the first visible action, and begin before the plan feels perfect.
  • Reading principle: Read first for structure and argument, then spend attention where evidence is most useful.
