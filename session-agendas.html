Session 4: Make Learning Stick.

You already have a strategy for reading difficult material. Today you’ll use it again—then learn how to turn the reading into selective notes, retrieval practice, and memory that lasts.

READ → NOTE → RETRIEVE → REPAIR → SPACE

0–8 minutes

Reactivate the three-step reading method

Use a fresh AP U.S. History passage to run another rep of the reading process you started last session:

  1. Map: identify the structure.
  2. Frame: state the central argument.
  3. Gather: read for evidence, causes, effects, contrasts, and qualifications.

The method is familiar now. The new question is: what should you actually keep from the reading?

8–22 minutes

Decide what is worth a note

Use the Worth a Note? challenge to separate high-value information from details that do not deserve equal attention.

Then build selective notes around six targets:

  • Main claim
  • Strong evidence
  • Cause and effect
  • Contrast or qualification
  • Terms worth knowing
  • Why it matters

Working rule: Notes are a filter, not a transcript.

22–36 minutes

Turn notes into retrieval prompts

Convert the notes into questions that make you produce the learning without looking. Build prompts for:

  • the argument
  • evidence
  • cause/effect
  • complexity
  • an AP-style connection

The goal is to create questions that require explanation and recall—not just recognition.

36–50 minutes

Run a retrieval round

Close the notes and answer the prompts from memory. After each response, reveal the reference notes and score yourself:

  • Got it — you can produce and explain it.
  • Partial — part of the idea is missing or weak.
  • Missed — you could not retrieve it accurately.

The site automatically moves Partial and Missed prompts into your Repair List.

50–56 minutes

Repair the gaps

Instead of rereading everything, focus only on the ideas that failed during retrieval.

Repair cycle: Check the missing information → explain it in your own words → close the notes → retrieve it again.

56–60 minutes

Space it and transfer it

Set a lightweight retrieval rhythm: today, tomorrow, several days later, and about a week later.

Then identify one real class where you can use the reusable Study Cycle.

Your study rule: Read strategically → take selective notes → create prompts → retrieve → repair → come back later.

Where you are now

From comprehension to retention

Last session focused on approaching demanding assignments and readings efficiently. This session extends that skill into what happens after you understand the material.

The key shift

Recognition is not recall

Something can feel familiar when you reread it and still be difficult to produce on a test. Retrieval practice gives you evidence that the learning is actually available.
