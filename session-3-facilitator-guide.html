Before the session

Protect the experiment

  • Open this guide and Isa’s page in separate windows.
  • Round A must use her normal method; do not coach the three-phase system early.
  • Score accuracy, reasoning, and transfer—not handwriting or polish.
  • Do not reveal keys until each retrieval stage is complete.
  • The student page is fully client-facing; keep this guide private.

Timing

Target 60 minutes, but protect the retrieve-and-repair stages if time compresses. Those are the instructional core.

Stage 1

Launch 4–5 minutes

Suggested script

“Today we are going to test two learning workflows with related Algebra 2 ideas. First, you will learn with your usual approach. Then you will use a three-phase process. We will let recall, explanation, application, and transfer decide what is worth keeping.”

Ask

  • What makes a study method earn a place in your system?
  • Which method do you predict will perform better?
  • How accurately do you usually judge whether you truly know something?

Avoid

Do not imply that the three-phase method is guaranteed to win. The legitimacy of the experiment matters.

Stage 2

Baseline: Quadratic forms 10 minutes

Mini-lesson script

A quadratic can be written in three common forms, and each form makes different information visible.

  • Standard form: y = ax² + bx + c. The y-intercept is c, and the sign of a shows whether the parabola opens up or down.
  • Factored form: y = a(x − r₁)(x − r₂). The zeros are r₁ and r₂, so the x-intercepts are (r₁,0) and (r₂,0).
  • Vertex form: y = a(x − h)² + k. The vertex is (h,k), the axis of symmetry is x = h, and the sign of a determines whether the vertex is a minimum or maximum.

Use examples: y = 2(x − 3)(x + 1) and y = −3(x + 2)² + 5.

Delivery

  • Teach for roughly four minutes.
  • Let Isa use her normal notes.
  • Close notes before assessment.
  • Have her record confidence before scoring.

Baseline key

  1. Standard: y-intercept c; opening from sign of a; coefficients available for later calculations.
  2. Factored: zeros/x-intercepts r₁ and r₂.
  3. Vertex: vertex (h,k), axis x = h, max/min from sign of a.
  4. x-intercepts 3 and −1; opens upward because a = 2.
  5. Vertex (−2,5); axis x = −2; maximum because a = −3.

Stage 3

Prime before class 9–10 minutes

Frame it

“The goal is not to teach yourself the whole lesson. It is to build enough structure that the explanation has somewhere to land.”

Coach the sequence

  1. Scan the quadratic formula and locate b² − 4ac.
  2. Activate prior knowledge about roots and x-intercepts.
  3. Predict why the sign of the square-root expression might matter.
  4. Attempt the pretest without correction first.
  5. Set one confirmation target, one confusion, one question, and one needed example.

Pretest key

  • x² − 5x + 6: Δ = 25 − 24 = 1; likely two real roots and two x-intercepts.
  • x² + 4x + 8: Δ = 16 − 32 = −16; roots are nonreal complex.

Do not overcorrect

Incorrect predictions are useful attention targets. Preserve them for comparison after instruction.

Stage 4

Process during class 12 minutes

Main lesson script

The discriminant is Δ = b² − 4ac, the expression under the square root in the quadratic formula.

  1. If Δ > 0, √Δ is a positive real number. The ± produces two distinct real roots. The graph crosses the x-axis twice.
  2. If Δ = 0, √Δ = 0. The ± no longer creates two different values, so there is one repeated real root. The parabola touches the x-axis once.
  3. If Δ < 0, the square root requires an imaginary number. There are two nonreal complex roots and no real x-intercepts.

Examples:

  • x² − 7x + 10: Δ = 49 − 40 = 9 → two real roots.
  • x² + 6x + 9: Δ = 36 − 36 = 0 → one repeated root.
  • 2x² + 4x + 7: Δ = 16 − 56 = −40 → two complex roots.

Nuance: Negative discriminant does not mean “no solutions.” It means no real solutions. Complex solutions still exist.

Selective-note coaching

Require Isa to capture the claim, why the sign matters, one example of each case, the graphical connection, and one misconception. If she begins transcribing, ask: “What is the structure you need later?”

Stage 5

Retrieve before review 10 minutes

Transition

“Now we remove the support. The goal is to discover what your brain can produce, not what looks familiar when the notes are open.”

Facilitation

  • Keep notes closed.
  • Allow silence and effort.
  • Require explanation of why the sign matters.
  • Record confidence before checking.

Retrieval key

  1. x² − 7x + 10: Δ = 9; two distinct real roots; two x-intercepts.
  2. x² + 6x + 9: Δ = 0; one repeated real root; one tangent x-intercept.
  3. 2x² + 4x + 7: Δ = −40; two nonreal complex roots; no x-intercepts.
  4. Graph connection as above.
  5. “No real roots” is accurate because complex roots remain.

Stage 6

Repair 8 minutes

Repair sequence

  1. Reveal the reference only after retrieval.
  2. Identify what was missing and inaccurate.
  3. Classify the gap.
  4. Name the cue that should trigger the correct concept next time.
  5. Convert remaining uncertainty into a precise teacher question.

Prevent decorative correction

Do not let her merely copy the key. Ask, “Why did your original model fail, and what signal would help you catch it next time?”

Strong teacher-question model

“I understand that a negative discriminant produces complex roots because the quadratic formula requires the square root of a negative number. I am unsure how the graph and complex roots relate when there are no x-intercepts. What graphical information, if any, represents those roots?”

Stage 7

Transfer 9 minutes

Answer key

  1. Any perfect-square quadratic works, such as x² − 6x + 9 = 0; Δ = 36 − 36 = 0.
  2. For x² + 4x + c, Δ = 16 − 4c. No real roots require 16 − 4c < 0, so c > 4. Example: c = 5.
  3. For x² + kx + 9, Δ = k² − 36. Two distinct real roots require k² > 36, so k < −6 or k > 6.
  4. Negative discriminant means no real roots, but two complex conjugate roots remain.

What to probe

  • Can she construct rather than only classify?
  • Can she reason with an inequality?
  • Can she explain a parameter condition?
  • Can she correct imprecise language?

Stage 8

Compare and decide 6–7 minutes

Debrief

  • Which method produced stronger recall and explanation?
  • Which exposed hidden gaps?
  • Did greater mental effort correspond to better performance?
  • Which phase added the most value?
  • What is the smallest realistic version for a school day?

Do not force adoption

Valid outcomes include adopt, modify, use selectively, retest, or reject. The method must earn its place.

Closeout

Field test and parent outcome

Between-session assignment

Use the cycle once with SAT preparation, Mandarin, or another unfamiliar topic. Track time, confidence, delayed recall, and one modification.

Parent-facing summary

Isa tested a research-aligned workflow for learning fast-moving material. Using Algebra 2 content, she compared her usual learning approach with a three-phase process involving advance preparation, selective note-taking, closed-note retrieval, corrective feedback, and transfer. The session produced individualized evidence about which elements improve her learning efficiency and accuracy.
