Learning at Speed: Facilitator Guide

A direct, practical lesson in what Isa should do before, during, and after class.

Before the session

Keep the lesson direct

  • No baseline comparison.
  • No math lesson or content test.
  • Teach each method plainly, model it briefly, and have Isa adapt it.
  • Use examples from several subjects so the method feels transferable.
  • Protect time for building a realistic minimum version.

Main risk

Do not turn the system into a complicated checklist. The objective is a small repeatable routine, not perfect compliance with every prompt.

Part 1

Explain the system 6 minutes

Suggested script

“The problem in a fast class is often not ability. It is that all of the learning is expected to happen while the teacher is moving. We are going to spread the work across three short moments: before class, during class, and after class. Each moment has a different job.”

  • Before class prepares attention.
  • During class captures structure.
  • After class strengthens memory and exposes gaps.

Ask

What do you currently do at each of those three points? Which point is mostly missing?

Part 2

Teach the before-class method 10 minutes

Teach

“A preview is not studying the whole lesson. It is a four-minute attention setup.”

  1. Scan: title, headings, bold terms, diagrams, formulas, examples, or assigned questions.
  2. Connect: name one thing already known that may connect.
  3. Question: write one question for class to answer.

Model across subjects

  • History: scan headings and ask what caused an event.
  • Science: scan the diagram and ask how one variable affects another.
  • English: scan the prompt and ask what evidence the author uses.
  • Math: scan the worked example and ask why a particular step is valid.

Correct overuse

If Isa turns the preview into full study, bring her back to: “Map, connection, question.”

Part 3

Teach the during-class method 14 minutes

Teach

“Your notes do not need to capture the class. They need to help you reconstruct the class later.”

Capture five things:

  1. Main idea
  2. How or why
  3. Example
  4. Contrast, exception, or limitation
  5. Unresolved question

When you feel behind, stop chasing sentences and return to the main idea.

Mini-practice

Give a one-minute explanation of any familiar topic—such as why sleep affects performance, how a school rule works, or how a current event developed. Ask Isa to take only five-part notes. Then review whether she captured structure rather than wording.

Watch for

  • Transcription
  • Decorating notes during instruction
  • Recording examples without the principle
  • Leaving confusion unmarked

Part 4

Teach the after-class method 16 minutes

Teach

“After class, memory comes before notes.”

  1. Close the notes.
  2. Explain the lesson aloud or on paper.
  3. Open the notes and compare.
  4. Correct the missing or inaccurate part.
  5. Try that part again without looking.
  6. Turn remaining confusion into a precise question.

Mini-practice

Use the one-minute explanation from the note-taking practice. Have Isa close the notes and reconstruct it. Then compare, repair, and formulate one question. This demonstrates the method without adding academic content.

Teacher-question frame

“I understand ______. I lose the reasoning when ______. I already tried ______. My question is ______.”

Do not accept passive checking

After correction, ask her to close the source and produce the corrected piece again. Otherwise the repair remains recognition rather than learning.

Part 5

Build Isa’s routine 10 minutes

Full version

  • Before: four-minute scan, connection, and question.
  • During: five-part selective notes.
  • After: closed-note explanation, check, correction, and question.

Minimum version

  • Before: one question.
  • During: main idea, one example, one question.
  • After: one closed-note explanation, then check.

Decision standard

Her version should be small enough to use on a tiring day. It is better to execute a reduced routine consistently than abandon an ideal routine.

Closeout

Field test

Choose one real course or learning task. Test the routine once and discuss:

  • Was class easier to follow?
  • Could she explain more afterward?
  • Which part was useful?
  • Which part needs to shrink or change?

Parent-facing summary

Isa learned a practical three-part system for handling fast-moving instruction. She practiced previewing material with a purpose, taking selective notes that capture lesson structure, and using closed-note retrieval and correction after class. She also built a minimum version designed for realistic use on busy school days.
