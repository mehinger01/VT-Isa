Before the session

Teach the methods—not a content lesson

  • There is no math, science, or history lesson to master.
  • The content of the session is the learning methods themselves.
  • For each method, teach: what it is, why it works, when to use it, how to execute it, and what weak substitute to avoid.
  • Use brief examples from multiple subjects only to clarify transfer.
  • End by helping Isa create strategy-selection rules.

Main risk

Do not collapse all methods into a generic study routine. Retrieval, spacing, self-explanation, worked examples, interleaving, and feedback solve different learning problems.

Opening

Exposure is not learning 6 minutes

Suggested script

“A lot of studying creates familiarity. You see the page again, recognize the answer, or follow someone else’s explanation. That can feel fluent without proving that you can produce or use the knowledge. Today we are going to learn the methods that create stronger mental work.”

“The key question is not, ‘How long did I study?’ It is, ‘What can I produce, explain, choose, or apply without support?’”

Ask

  • Which study activities currently make you feel productive?
  • Which of those actually require you to produce something without help?
  • Have you ever felt prepared and then blanked on a test?

Method 1

Retrieval practice 9 minutes

Teach

“Retrieval means attempting to pull information from memory before looking. The attempt strengthens access and reveals gaps that rereading can hide.”

  1. Close the source.
  2. Produce the idea by writing, speaking, drawing, or solving.
  3. Check the source.
  4. Correct the gap.
  5. Try the missed part again without looking.

Examples

  • Vocabulary: see the term, produce the definition and an example.
  • Science: redraw and label a process from memory.
  • History: reconstruct causes and effects on a blank page.
  • Writing: state the argument and supporting evidence without rereading.

Correct these errors

  • Recognition-only flashcards
  • Rereading before attempting recall
  • Checking too quickly
  • Correcting without retrying

Method 2

Spacing 7 minutes

Teach

“Spacing means revisiting learning over multiple days. The slight forgetting between attempts makes retrieval effortful enough to strengthen memory.”

A usable starting schedule is next day, 2–3 days later, and about one week later. Reviews should be brief retrieval attempts, not repeated rereading sessions.

Distinguish

Massed practice: twenty minutes repeated tonight.
Spaced practice: several shorter retrievals distributed across days.

Nuance

Spacing is not merely putting “study” on a calendar. The spaced event must contain active retrieval, explanation, or application.

Method 3

Self-explanation and deep questions 9 minutes

Teach

“Self-explanation connects facts into a model. A strong explanation includes the claim, mechanism, connection, example, and boundary.”

  • What is the main claim?
  • Why is it true?
  • How does it work?
  • What does it connect to?
  • What is an example and a non-example?
  • When does it not apply?

Model the difference

Weak: “Photosynthesis is how plants make food.”
Stronger: explains inputs, energy transformation, outputs, where it occurs, and a boundary such as conditions that limit the process.

Avoid oversimplification

Do not remove necessary terminology in the name of making an explanation simple. Precision is part of understanding.

Method 4

Worked examples with fading 8 minutes

Teach

“When a procedure is new, unsupported practice can overload attention. Begin with a correct model, explain each step, then remove support gradually.”

  1. Complete example
  2. Example with final steps removed
  3. Example with more steps removed
  4. Independent attempt
  5. Feedback and correction

Cross-subject uses

  • Chemistry calculations
  • Mathematical procedures
  • Citation formatting
  • Lab procedures
  • Paragraph or essay structures
  • Grammar analysis

Two opposite mistakes

Passive example watching gives too little mental work. Immediate unsupported problem solving can give too much difficulty before a usable model exists.

Methods 5–6

Interleaving, feedback, and correction 10 minutes

Interleaving

“Blocked practice tells you what method to use because every problem is the same type. Interleaving mixes related types so you must diagnose the situation and choose.”

Use it when the challenge is recognizing which concept, procedure, grammar rule, or problem type applies.

Feedback and correction

“Feedback is useful only when it changes the mental model or future action.”

  1. Name what was wrong.
  2. Explain why it was wrong.
  3. Identify the cue that was missed.
  4. State the corrected rule.
  5. Try a new variation.
  6. Retest later.

Avoid

Ten identical problems do not develop method selection. Copying a correct answer into an error log does not prove the error has been repaired.

Final teaching segment

Match method to demand 8 minutes

Teach these starting rules

  • Facts and vocabulary: spaced retrieval with production and feedback.
  • Concepts: retrieval plus self-explanation, examples, non-examples, and representations.
  • New procedures: worked examples followed by faded support.
  • Choosing a method: interleaving and comparison.
  • Transfer: varied application and unfamiliar cases.
  • Recurring errors: precise feedback, corrected rule, new variation, delayed retest.

Suggested close

“The goal is not to use every method every time. Diagnose what the task demands and choose the smallest method that creates the right mental work.”

Check for false rules

Do not let the choice be based on which method feels easiest or matches a supposed learning style. Choose based on the required performance.

Closeout

Personal strategy playbook

Have Isa complete four rules:

  • When I need to remember facts, I will…
  • When I need to understand a concept, I will…
  • When I need to learn a procedure, I will…
  • When I make an error, I will…

Parent-facing summary

Isa received direct instruction in evidence-based learning methods, including retrieval practice, spacing, self-explanation, worked examples with faded support, interleaving, and corrective feedback. She learned that different academic demands require different strategies and created starting rules for selecting an effective method rather than relying on rereading, highlighting, copying, or repeated identical practice.
