Before the session

Set up the experiment

  • Open this guide on your screen and Isa’s lesson in a separate tab or window.
  • Do not reveal the answer keys or your predictions before she completes each task.
  • Protect the comparison: Round A uses her normal method; Round B uses the structured system.
  • Score performance based on accuracy, explanation, and transfer—not neatness.
  • Keep direct teaching brief. The value comes from performance and reflection.

Central discipline

Do not rescue too quickly. Productive struggle during retrieval is part of the learning mechanism. Intervene when confusion becomes unproductive, not merely uncomfortable.

Stage 1

Launch the experiment 5 minutes

Suggested script

“Today we are not going to assume a study method works because it sounds good. We are going to compare two learning workflows using similar math and let your performance decide. First, you will learn using your normal approach. Then you will use a three-phase system. We will compare recall, explanation, application, confidence, and transfer.”

Ask

  • Which method do you predict will work better?
  • What makes a learning method worth keeping?
  • How accurately do you normally judge whether you know something?

Avoid

Do not frame the three-phase method as obviously superior. Isa’s ownership depends on the evidence feeling legitimate.

Stage 2

Baseline lesson 10 minutes

Mini-lesson script

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers. An explicit rule calculates any term directly from its position. A recursive rule calculates a term using one or more previous terms and must include a starting value.

Use: 5, 8, 11, 14, ...

  • Explicit: aₙ = 5 + 3(n − 1)
  • Recursive: a₁ = 5 and aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + 3, n ≥ 2

Analogy: explicit is a direct street address; recursive is step-by-step directions from the previous location.

Delivery directions

  • Teach for roughly four minutes.
  • Let Isa take notes however she normally would.
  • Close the notes before the assessment.
  • Have her estimate confidence before scoring.

Baseline key

  1. Explicit uses n directly; recursive depends on prior terms and initial value(s).
  2. aₙ = 7 + 4(n − 1), or 4n + 3.
  3. a₁ = 7; aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + 4.
  4. Explicit is more efficient for the 80th term.
  5. aₙ = 20 − 6(n − 1), or 26 − 6n.

Stage 3

Prime before class 10 minutes

Frame the phase

“The goal is not to master the lesson before class. The goal is to create enough structure that the teacher’s explanation has somewhere to land.”

Coach these moves

  1. Scan the two sequences, formulas, and vocabulary.
  2. Activate prior knowledge about linear and exponential change.
  3. Predict what each formula means.
  4. Attempt the pretest before teaching.
  5. Identify one idea to confirm, one confusion, one question, and one needed example.

Do not overcorrect

Let incorrect predictions remain visible. They become attention targets during instruction and useful evidence during repair.

Expected pretest

Arithmetic is additive because differences are +5. Geometric is multiplicative because ratios are ×3. The eighth arithmetic term is 39.

Stage 4

Process during class 12 minutes

Main lesson script

Central distinction: arithmetic sequences add the same amount each step; geometric sequences multiply by the same factor each step.

  1. 4, 9, 14, 19 has constant difference +5.
  2. aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d because reaching term n requires n − 1 additive jumps.
  3. 4, 12, 36, 108 has constant ratio 3.
  4. gₙ = g₁rⁿ⁻¹ because reaching term n requires n − 1 multiplications.
  5. Arithmetic sequence points align on a line; geometric points generally curve.
  6. Decision rule: test differences, then ratios.

Worked examples: 6, 10, 14, 18 gives aₙ = 6 + 4(n − 1), a₁₀ = 42. 5, 15, 45, 135 gives gₙ = 5·3ⁿ⁻¹, g₆ = 1215.

Boundary: a nonzero constant sequence can be both arithmetic and geometric.

Require selective notes

Make sure she records the claim, mechanism, example, boundary, representation, and unresolved question. Interrupt transcription gently: “What is the structure here—not every sentence?”

Stage 5

Retrieve before review 10 minutes

Transition script

“Now we remove the support. The goal is not to feel fluent. The goal is to discover what your brain can actually produce.”

Facilitation

  • Keep notes closed.
  • Allow silence and effort.
  • Ask her to explain n − 1, not merely reproduce it.
  • Require one self-created example of each family.
  • Record confidence before checking.

Retrieval key

  1. 18, 13, 8, 3 is arithmetic, d = −5; aₙ = 18 − 5(n − 1).
  2. 2, 6, 18, 54 is geometric, r = 3; gₙ = 2·3ⁿ⁻¹.
  3. Term 1 has undergone zero changes; term n has undergone n − 1.
  4. Arithmetic points are linear; geometric points generally curve.
  5. 7, 7, 7, 7 works as both, with d = 0 and r = 1.

Stage 6

Repair the model 8 minutes

Repair sequence

  1. Reveal the student reference only after retrieval is complete.
  2. Identify missing and inaccurate information.
  3. Classify the main gap.
  4. Name the cue that should trigger the correct idea next time.
  5. Turn residual confusion into a precise teacher question.

Watch for decorative correction

Do not let Isa merely copy the right answer. Ask: “Why did your original model fail, and what would help you recognize this next time?”

Strong teacher-question model

“I understand that arithmetic sequences have constant differences and geometric sequences have constant ratios. I am unsure how to classify a sequence when both tests appear to work. Are constant sequences the only overlap?”

Stage 7

Transfer challenge 8 minutes

Answer key

  1. Plan A: Aₙ = 20 + 10(n − 1). Plan B: Bₙ = 20(1.5)ⁿ⁻¹. Week 6 values: 70 and 151.875.
  2. 3, 6 can continue arithmetically as 9, 12 or geometrically as 12, 24. Two terms do not determine a unique family.
  3. 2, 4, 8, 16 is geometric because ratios are constantly 2; differences are not constant.
  4. 5, 5, 5, 5 has d = 0 and r = 1.

Transfer coaching

Ask, “What structure stayed the same even when the story or numbers changed?” Score reasoning and classification cues, not merely final numbers.

Stage 8

Compare and decide 7 minutes

Debrief questions

  • Which approach produced stronger recall?
  • Which exposed more hidden gaps?
  • Did the harder-feeling method perform better?
  • Which phase added the most value?
  • Which part would be unrealistic on a normal school day?
  • What is the smallest version that would retain most of the benefit?

Do not force adoption

The legitimate outcomes are adopt, modify, use selectively, retest, or reject. A sophisticated program allows the strategy to fail its audition.

Closeout

Convert the experiment into a field test

Between-session assignment

Use the cycle once with SAT preparation, Mandarin, or another unfamiliar topic. Track time, confidence, delayed recall, and one necessary modification.

Parent-facing summary

Isa tested a research-aligned workflow designed for fast-paced courses. She practiced preparing before instruction, capturing lesson structure, retrieving without notes, correcting gaps, and applying learning in unfamiliar situations. The session generated individualized evidence about which elements improve her learning efficiency.
